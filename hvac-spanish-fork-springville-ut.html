Spanish Fork and Springville sit side by side at the south end of Utah Valley, and they’re growing at very different speeds. Spanish Fork reached about 50,282 residents in 2025, up about 18% from 42,682 in April 2020, and added about 2,850 housing units. Springville grew about 4%, from 35,268 to 36,523. Salem, just south, grew about 31% to 12,304 (Utah Population Committee 2025 estimates). This page collects the local facts we could source for all three: housing, climate, the city power utilities, and permits.

Housing data

The 2000s were the biggest building decade in both cities: 28% of Spanish Fork’s housing units and 24% of Springville’s. Spanish Fork’s median home dates to 2001 and Springville’s to 1996. Springville has the older core: about 40% of its units predate 1990, compared with 31% in Spanish Fork. Both are mostly owner-occupied, Spanish Fork more so (80% vs 72%), and about 84% of occupied homes in each city heat with utility gas (ACS tables B25034, B25035, B25003, B25040).

What the numbers suggest

General context, not a diagnosis of your system:

  • Homes from 1990 through 2009 (45% in Spanish Fork, 40% in Springville): these homes are now roughly 17 to 36 years old. Furnace makers commonly cite a 15-to-20-year typical lifespan (American Standard), so a home that still has its original furnace is at or past that range. See repair or replace an original furnace in a 1990s–2001 home and our furnace replacement guide.
  • Homes before 1980: about 33% of Springville’s units and 25% of Spanish Fork’s. Replacing a furnace in an older house raises venting, sizing, and ductwork questions; see replacing the furnace in a 1970s–80s home.
  • Homes from 2010 or later (25% in Spanish Fork): the furnace may still be under manufacturer warranty. Check before you pay: how new-home furnace warranties work.
  • Heating fuel: about 14–15% of occupied homes in both cities heat with electricity. If you’re weighing a heat pump, see AC and heat pump replacement, and read the utility section below first.

If heat is out right now, start with no-heat help.

A colder station than Provo

NOAA has a climate-normals station in Spanish Fork, the Spanish Fork Power House, east of the city center at about 4,720 feet. Its 1991–2020 normals show about 5,415 heating degree days a year, with 1,080 in January and 1,068 in December, and an average January low of 22.0°F (NOAA annual normals; monthly). That’s a heavier heating load than the Provo BYU station, which shows about 4,968 heating degree days and a 23.8°F January low (NOAA, Provo BYU). Cooling is lighter: about 1,013 cooling degree days at Spanish Fork vs about 1,205 at Provo. Springville has no normals station, so treat these as a regional guide.

City power, and what it means for rebates

This is where the two cities differ from most of the Wasatch Front, and from each other:

  • Spanish Fork runs its own electric utility, Spanish Fork Power & Light. Outages: 801-804-4550 (Spanish Fork Power & Light). Spanish Fork and Salem are members of the Utah Municipal Power Agency, along with Provo (UMPA member cities; RenewChoice communities).
  • Provo’s heat pump rebate doesn’t carry over. UMPA cities share the RenewChoice program, but its Conserve page says “currently, only Provo City is offering this rebate” and that other cities may offer it in the future (RenewChoice). We didn’t find an HVAC rebate on Spanish Fork Power & Light’s page when we checked on November 2, 2026. Ask the utility before you count on one. If you’re comparing notes with someone in Provo, see how the Provo rebate works.
  • Springville runs its own utility too, Springville City Power, (801) 489-2750 (Springville City Power). It isn’t on UMPA’s member list. Its Resources page has a “Rebates” link that goes to UAMPS Smart Energy (Springville Power resources), which lists programs for heat pumps, ductless heat pumps, gas furnaces, central AC, and smart thermostats, but says participation “differs by measure and by city,” so confirm with your utility before you buy (UAMPS Smart Energy).
  • Wattsmart likely doesn’t apply. Rocky Mountain Power’s Wattsmart rebates require that you buy your electricity from Rocky Mountain Power (Wattsmart). Check the name on your electric bill.
  • Gas: if your gas bill is from Enbridge Gas, its ThermWise program lists furnace and dual-fuel heat pump rebates (ThermWise). Our furnace replacement guide summarizes them.
  • Federal: the 25C tax credit isn’t allowed for equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025 (IRS).

Permits

  • Spanish Fork: the city lists “water heaters and HVAC equipment” among the work that needs a permit, and names HVAC replacements as a common residential project. Permits and inspections go through the CitizenServe portal; inspections must be scheduled at least 24 hours ahead, and if the work isn’t ready, a $93 re-inspection fee may apply. Inspection scheduling: 801-804-4540 (Spanish Fork Building).
  • Springville: the Building Division handles permits and schedules inspections through City Inspect or 801.489.2704. Following Utah Code, it provides an inspection within three business days of a request, and it recommends scheduling 48 hours ahead. It also publishes a “Work Exempt From Permits” list (Springville Building Division).
  • Salem: permits go through Salem City.

Most homeowners have the contractor pull the permit. Ask whether it’s included in the bid and who schedules the inspection.

Check the license before work starts

Before anyone works on your system, look the company up on DOPL’s free Licensee Lookup & Verification. You want an Active status and an HVAC classification (H100, or the older S350), under the same business name that’s on your estimate. Step-by-step: how to check a Utah HVAC license.

FAQ

Do you cover Spanish Fork and Springville?

You can send a free request from any Spanish Fork, Springville, or Salem ZIP. Matching depends on licensed Utah HVAC shops that serve your ZIP, so we can’t promise a match for every address, and we don’t name or vouch for particular shops. If a match is made, those pros may reach out.

Who provides electricity in Spanish Fork and Springville?

Both cities run their own electric utilities: Spanish Fork Power & Light and Springville City Power. Check the name on your bill. Rocky Mountain Power’s Wattsmart rebates require Rocky Mountain Power electricity.

Does Provo’s heat pump rebate work in Spanish Fork?

No. Spanish Fork and Salem share the RenewChoice program with Provo through UMPA, but RenewChoice says only Provo City currently offers the Conserve heat pump rebate. Ask Spanish Fork Power & Light whether anything is planned.

Are there HVAC rebates for Springville City Power customers?

Springville’s power Resources page links its Rebates button to UAMPS Smart Energy, which lists heat pump, furnace, AC, and thermostat programs but says participation differs by measure and by city. Confirm with Springville City Power at (801) 489-2750 before you buy.

Do I need a permit to replace a furnace or AC in Spanish Fork?

Yes. Spanish Fork lists water heaters and HVAC equipment among work that needs a permit, applied for through CitizenServe. Most homeowners have the contractor pull it.

How cold does Spanish Fork get?

NOAA’s 1991–2020 normals for the Spanish Fork Power House station show an average January low of 22.0°F and about 5,415 heating degree days a year, more than the Provo BYU station’s 4,968.
