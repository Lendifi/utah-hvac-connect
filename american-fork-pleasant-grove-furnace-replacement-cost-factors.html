Two cities, two housing mixes

The two cities have nearly the same number of homes and almost the same median build year, but very different mixes. American Fork is split at both ends: about a third of its housing units were built before 1980 and another third in 2010 or later, with only 12% from the 2000s. Pleasant Grove is spread more evenly, with about a quarter in each era and its biggest share from 1980 through 2009 (ACS 2020–2024). That matters because the era of the home often decides which scope questions show up in a bid:

  • Before 1980: the furnace has likely been replaced at least once. Venting, an old chimney, and older ductwork are the usual questions. See replacing the furnace in a 1970s–80s home.
  • 1980 through 1999: often an original furnace or a first replacement. Moving from a non-condensing furnace to a 90%-plus model means PVC venting, a condensate drain, and possibly a water heater chimney liner. See what changes in a 1990s–2001 home.
  • 2000 through 2009: an original AC may use R-22, and a new AC usually means a matched indoor coil. See early-2000s cost factors.
  • 2010 or later: check the warranty before paying for parts. See builder-grade furnace warranties.

Home type and where the equipment sits

About 12% of American Fork’s housing units and 11% of Pleasant Grove’s are single-family attached homes, and about a fifth in each city are in buildings with five or more units (ACS table B25024). In attached homes and condos, where the furnace sits (a closet, an attic, a garage) and where the outdoor unit can go shape the labor and any HOA approval. Ask about access and any HOA rules before you compare bids.

Heating fuel matters too. About 82% of occupied homes in American Fork and 85% in Pleasant Grove heat with utility gas; 16% and 12% heat with electricity (ACS table B25040). If your home heats with electricity now, the comparison is different. Start with AC and heat pump replacement.

City permit fees: how each city charges

American Fork bases building permit fees on the valuation of the work, using a city table (American Fork, Building Permit Fees; valuation table). That means the permit fee grows with the declared value of the job. The city also lists:

  • a plan review fee based on valuation, with a $50 minimum for permits other than new homes;
  • a state fee of 1% of the building permit fee;
  • a 3% credit card processing fee on permit payments over $300 (American Fork);
  • in its fee schedule effective July 1, 2026: a residential reinspection fee of $175 per unit, an inspection cancellation fee of $125 per unit for any scheduled inspection, and a $50 gas line review (American Fork FY 2026–2027 fee schedule).

The reinspection and cancellation fees are the ones a homeowner can feel: a failed or missed inspection costs real money in American Fork. Ask who pays them if it happens.

Pleasant Grove works differently. After an application is submitted, a permit technician reviews it and calls to collect a submittal (plan check) fee, which is applied toward the total cost of the building permit. Applications need a contractor’s license number or an Owner-Builder Certification Form (Pleasant Grove Building Division). The city’s fee schedule page lists a $75 submittal fee for “All Others (Solar, Signs, Basements)” (Pleasant Grove fee schedule). It doesn’t name HVAC work, so ask the city or your contractor which category a furnace permit falls under. Cedar Hills shares ZIP 84062 but issues its own permits (see our Pleasant Grove page).

Rules that change the scope

  • Federal minimums. New non-weatherized gas furnaces have had to reach 80% AFUE since November 19, 2015, and, as codified today, must reach 95% for units made on and after December 18, 2028 (10 CFR 430.32(e)). A home with a metal flue that moves to a condensing furnace has venting work in the bid. More in our guide to the 2028 rule.
  • Rebates can change the net, not the bid. Enbridge Gas ThermWise lists furnace rebates for 95% AFUE and higher, and the request must be postmarked within six months of installation (ThermWise). Rocky Mountain Power’s Wattsmart dual-fuel rebate requires Rocky Mountain Power electricity and a program-participating contractor (Wattsmart). Rocky Mountain Power’s service map lists both cities (RMP service area map), but check the name on your bill. Current amounts are on each program’s page.

How to compare bids in American Fork or Pleasant Grove

  • Same scope on every bid, with model numbers and the AHRI reference number for any matched furnace, coil, and AC.
  • Permit included? Which city issues it (American Fork, Pleasant Grove, or Cedar Hills), and is the city fee in the bid?
  • Inspections. Who schedules and attends them, and who pays an American Fork reinspection or cancellation fee if one happens?
  • Venting, liner, and drain for an older home; coil for an R-22-era AC; warranty for a newer home.
  • Access and HOA for attached homes and condos.
  • Haul-away and rebate paperwork: listed, and who files.
  • License: check every bidder on DOPL (below).

FAQ

How much does a furnace replacement cost in American Fork?

We don’t publish prices; your real number comes from licensed contractors who see the home. What moves a bid: the home’s era (venting, coil, warranty), access and HOA rules, efficiency and sizing, and city permit fees. Get at least two itemized bids for the same scope.

How does American Fork calculate a permit fee?

American Fork bases building permit fees on the valuation of the work using a city table, adds a plan review fee with a $50 minimum for most permits, and a state fee of 1% of the permit fee. Its July 1, 2026 fee schedule lists a $175 residential reinspection fee and a $125 inspection cancellation fee.

What does Pleasant Grove charge to apply for a permit?

Pleasant Grove collects a submittal (plan check) fee after a permit technician reviews the application, and applies it toward the building permit. Its fee schedule page lists $75 for “All Others (Solar, Signs, Basements)”; ask whether a furnace permit falls in that category.

Why would two bids for the same furnace be so different?

Often because the scope differs: one includes new PVC venting, a chimney liner, a matched coil, the permit, or haul-away and the other doesn’t. Ask every bidder to list the same items with model numbers so you can compare.

Do rebates lower the price of a new furnace?

They can lower your net cost after the job, not the bid itself. ThermWise lists furnace rebates for 95% AFUE and higher, with requests postmarked within six months of installation. Wattsmart requires Rocky Mountain Power electricity. Check current amounts on each program’s page.

Are townhomes different?

They can be. About 12% of American Fork’s and 11% of Pleasant Grove’s housing units are single-family attached. Where the furnace and outdoor unit sit, access, and HOA rules can change the labor in a bid.
