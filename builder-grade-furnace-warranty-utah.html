Builder-grade furnace in a newer Utah home: check the warranty first

How manufacturer warranties treat new construction, what they leave out, and what to ask before you pay. We match; we don’t diagnose or quote.

By the Utah HVAC Connect editorial team · Published October 26, 2026 · Last updated October 26, 2026

TL;DR

  • In a newly built home, Carrier and Goodman both treat the date you bought the home from the builder as the furnace’s installation date.
  • Registration matters: Goodman asks for it within 60 days and Carrier within 90. Registered, both list 10 years of parts coverage; unregistered, 5.
  • Parts warranties don’t cover labor (Carrier excludes diagnosis, repair, and installation costs), and second owners get shorter terms.
  • About 29% of Utah County housing was built in 2010 or later, and more than half in ZIPs like Saratoga Springs, Eagle Mountain, and Herriman (ACS 2020–2024).
  • Before you approve a repair: find the model and serial number, your closing date, and the registration. Wattsmart heat pump rebates exclude new construction.

If you bought your home new, the furnace in it was chosen by the builder, not by you. People call that “builder-grade.” It isn’t a technical term and it doesn’t mean the furnace is bad; it just means you didn’t pick the model, and you may not have the paperwork. When something breaks, the first question isn’t repair or replace. It’s whether the part is still under warranty, and for how long. This guide explains how manufacturer furnace warranties treat newly built homes, using two published warranties as examples, and what to ask before you pay for a repair.

In a new home, the clock starts at closing

Both published warranties we read answer the new-construction question directly:

  • Carrier (condensing gas furnace warranty, April 2022 edition): “Where a product is installed in a newly constructed home, the date of installation is the date the homeowner purchased the home from the builder.” To get the longer original-owner terms, the furnace must be registered within 90 days of original installation; otherwise parts coverage is 5 years instead of 10. If the installation date can’t be verified, the warranty starts 90 days after the date of manufacture shown by the serial number (Carrier warranty certificate).
  • Goodman: “For units installed in a newly constructed residence, the installation date is the date the owner purchases the residence from the builder.” Online registration within 60 days of the installation date gets up to 10 years of parts coverage for as long as the original registered owner or spouse owns and lives in the home; otherwise the period is 5 years (Goodman).

Carrier’s certificate notes that in California, Quebec, and other places that prohibit tying warranty benefits to registration, the longer term applies without registering. We haven’t confirmed whether any Utah rule changes that, so plan on registering.

What a parts warranty does and doesn’t cover

  • Parts, not labor. Carrier’s certificate excludes “labor or other costs incurred for diagnosing, repairing, removing, installing” parts. A covered part can still come with a bill for the service call and labor, unless a builder, installer, or separate plan covers that. Ask what your builder’s or installer’s paperwork says.
  • Heat exchanger terms are often longer. On Carrier’s 59SC5 series, the heat exchanger is covered for life when registered (20 years if not). Goodman lists a lifetime heat exchanger limited warranty, good for as long as you own the home, with registration (Carrier; Goodman).
  • Conditions matter. Carrier’s certificate requires installation by a licensed HVAC technician, the product staying in its original location, and normal maintenance per the owner’s manual. It also excludes filter replacement and other normal maintenance, and damage from faulty installation or improper servicing.
  • Second owners get less. Carrier lists 5 years of parts coverage for subsequent owners, with no registration required. Goodman ties its 10-year term to the original registered owner or spouse. If you bought a newer home from its first owner, ask whether the furnace was registered and when the home first closed.

Find your paperwork in 15 minutes

  1. Find the model and serial number. Goodman notes that your installing dealer can help locate them, and that many dealers attach the “traveler label” from the shipping box to your installation paperwork (Goodman). Check your builder’s closing packet or warranty binder.
  2. Find your closing date. For a newly built home, both warranties above treat the day you bought the home from the builder as the installation date, so your closing documents may matter as proof.
  3. Check registration. Look for a registration confirmation email from the closing period, or ask the manufacturer or the original installer whether the serial number is registered. Goodman’s registration asks for the contractor’s name and phone number, so it helps to know who installed the furnace.
  4. Find the certificate for your model. Carrier’s says to keep it with your records; Goodman says certificates are available from dealers or its website.
  5. Read the builder’s warranty documents. Builders’ own warranties vary, and we don’t summarize them. Look for what they say about the heating system, and who to contact.

Before you approve a repair

  • Tell the shop the home’s closing date and that you think the furnace may be under warranty.
  • Ask which part failed, whether it’s covered, and what you’d pay for labor and the service call either way.
  • Ask whether the shop handles the warranty part claim. Carrier’s certificate says defective parts go back through a registered servicing dealer.
  • If the furnace short-cycles or shuts off early, our short-cycling guide covers what to note first. If it’s blowing cold air, see why a furnace blows cold air.
  • Replacement rarely comes up on a furnace this young, but if it does, read our furnace replacement guide first, and ask for the reason in writing.

Rebates and brand-new homes

Rocky Mountain Power’s Wattsmart dual-fuel and air-source heat pump rebates say the home must be an existing single-family home, not new construction (Wattsmart dual fuel; Wattsmart air-source heat pumps). For homes being built, the utility runs a separate Wattsmart New Homes program that works through builders and HERS-rated homes (Wattsmart New Homes). If you’re upgrading equipment in a home you already own and live in, ThermWise and Wattsmart rebates may apply as usual; our heat pump rebate summary lists them.

Check the license before work starts

Carrier’s warranty requires installation by a licensed HVAC technician. Before anyone works on your system, look the company up on DOPL’s free Licensee Lookup & Verification. You want an Active status and an HVAC classification (H100, or the older S350), under the same business name that’s on your estimate. Step-by-step: how to check a Utah HVAC license.

FAQ

When does a furnace warranty start in a brand-new home?

In the two warranties we read, it starts on the date you bought the home from the builder. Carrier and Goodman both treat that date as the installation date for a newly constructed home.

How long do I have to register a furnace?

It depends on the brand. Goodman asks for online registration within 60 days of the installation date, and Carrier within 90 days. Without registration, both list 5 years of parts coverage instead of 10.

Does a furnace warranty cover labor?

Carrier’s certificate excludes labor and other costs of diagnosing, repairing, removing, or installing parts. A builder, installer, or separate plan may cover labor; check your paperwork.

I bought a five-year-old home from its first owner. Is the furnace still covered?

Possibly, but on different terms. Carrier lists 5 years of parts coverage for subsequent owners, counted from the original installation. Goodman ties its 10-year term to the original registered owner or spouse. Ask the manufacturer with the serial number.

Can I get a Wattsmart rebate on a new home?

Wattsmart’s dual-fuel and air-source heat pump rebates are for existing single-family homes, not new construction. Rocky Mountain Power runs a separate Wattsmart New Homes program for builders.
