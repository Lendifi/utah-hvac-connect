Connect with licensed Utah shops for furnace replacement

Free match request for Salt Lake & Utah County homeowners researching a furnace or system replacement — we introduce licensed pros; we don’t install or quote

Utah’s climate is heating-heavy. Salt Lake City sees far more heating degree days than cooling degree days in a typical year (NOAA 1991–2020 normals), and most homes in Salt Lake and Utah counties rely on utility gas heat (about 79–80% per ACS 2024 1-year). That means furnace and system replacement conversations are a steady part of local HVAC demand — especially for 1990s–2000s housing stock now reaching common replacement-age windows along the south Salt Lake Valley and north Utah County corridor.

Utah HVAC Connect connects homeowners with licensed Utah HVAC contractors who serve your ZIP. We are a matchmaker under Apex Lead Group, LLC — not a contractor. We don’t sell equipment, perform installs, diagnose your system, or provide price quotes.

When homeowners typically look at replacement

Homeowners often start researching replacement when a furnace is aging, repairs are stacking up, efficiency feels poor, or they’re planning a remodel or sale. Many homeowners hear mid-teens to mid-twenties years of age as a common window when shops discuss options — treat that as soft guidance only, and confirm what applies to your equipment with a licensed contractor. We don’t set replacement rules.

Along the Wasatch Front, replacement shopping often ramps in the heating season (October–February) when failures show up, and in shoulder months when homeowners plan ahead. Peak cold stretches in December and January can make “repair now vs replace soon” a live topic with shops — again, their conversation, not our advice.

Repair vs replace — framing the shop conversation

We don’t tell you whether to repair or replace. Licensed shops assess condition, parts availability, safety, and your goals on site. Useful framing when you talk with them:

  • What you want in the short term (restore heat this week) vs longer term (fewer surprises next winters)
  • Rough system age and any recent major repairs you remember
  • Whether you’re open to furnace-only work, a full system discussion, or still deciding
  • Timeline: urgent, this month, or planning for later

If heat is already out, start with urgency on our winter no-heat path or pick “Today / This week” on the form. If you’re planning a replacement, say so — shops can respond with the right kind of follow-up.

What to include on the form

These details help shops understand replacement intent without turning our page into a quote calculator:

  • Service type — Replace, or “I’m not sure” if you’re still weighing repair
  • System age — Approximate is fine if you know it (supports clearer shop follow-up)
  • Timeline — Today, this week, this month, or planning
  • ZIP — So we can route to shops that serve your area
  • Contact window — When it’s reasonable for pros to reach you

We don’t need brand model numbers or photos for matching. Share whatever you’re comfortable with; shops will ask for more if they need it. Consent and contact rules live on the form and our TCPA disclosure — we don’t put a pre-submit “you’ll be called” promise on this page.
