What the numbers suggest

General context, not a diagnosis:

  • East Sandy (84092, 84093): in homes built in the late 1970s and 1980s, the furnace and AC have very likely been replaced at least once. What matters is the age of the current equipment, not the house. Older homes also bring venting and ductwork questions: moving from an older 80% AFUE furnace to a 90%-plus condensing model usually needs new PVC venting and a condensate drain. Ask whether the bid covers it.
  • Adding or upgrading cooling: some older homes were built with smaller or no central AC. If a shop suggests a heat pump, our AC and heat pump page explains dual fuel and the 2026 rebates.
  • Draper (84020): with a 2003 median, many original furnaces are now past the 15-to-20-year span furnace makers commonly cite (American Standard). See our repair-or-replace questions.

Heating season on the southeast bench

At Salt Lake City International Airport, NOAA’s 1991–2020 normals show about 5,154 heating degree days a year, from 337 in October to 1,018 in December and 1,041 in January (NOAA monthly normals; annual). The south valley and benches sit higher than the airport, so treat the airport as a regional guide rather than a reading for your street.

Utilities and rebates

  • Gas: Enbridge Gas ThermWise listed $300 for a 95%–97.4% AFUE gas furnace and $350 for 97.5% AFUE or higher with an ECM blower, plus $700–$1,200 for qualifying dual-fuel systems, on October 7, 2026 (ThermWise).
  • Electric: if your bill says Rocky Mountain Power, Wattsmart listed up to $1,450 for an AHRI-rated dual-fuel heat pump system ($700 if not AHRI-rated), installed by a participating contractor in an existing single-family home (Wattsmart).
  • Federal: the 25C tax credit isn’t allowed for equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025 (IRS).

Permits in Sandy and Draper

Sandy City’s residential mechanical permit guide lists an “A/C Furnace replacement” permit for “the replacement or addition of any HVAC system,” which includes a gas line upgrade; a separate gas line permit covers gas line work that isn’t already part of another permit, and a general mechanical permit covers other alterations or repairs (Sandy residential mechanical permits; Sandy building permits). In Draper, start with the city’s building permits page. Ask your contractor whether the permit is in the bid.

Check the license before work starts

Before anyone works on your system, look the company up on DOPL’s free Licensee Lookup & Verification. You want an Active status and an HVAC classification (H100, or the older S350), under the same business name that’s on your estimate. Step-by-step: how to check a Utah HVAC license.

FAQ

My east Sandy home is from the 1970s or ’80s. What should I ask an HVAC shop?

Ask how old the current furnace and AC are (they may not be original), what condition the venting and ductwork are in, and, if replacement comes up, whether the bid includes venting changes, any gas line work, the permit, and haul-away.

Do I need a permit to replace a furnace or AC in Sandy?

Yes. Sandy City lists an “A/C Furnace replacement” mechanical permit for the replacement or addition of any HVAC system. Contractors usually apply for it.

Is a heat pump a good idea for an older Sandy or Draper home?

It depends on the home, ductwork, and electrical panel, which a licensed shop assesses. Many Utah bids pair a heat pump with a gas furnace (dual fuel), and both Utah utilities list dual-fuel rebates.

Is Draper in Salt Lake County or Utah County?

Mostly Salt Lake County, with part of the city extending into Utah County. We cover both counties; enter your ZIP on the Get Help form.

Is the request free, and do you send the technician?

The request is free for homeowners with no obligation to hire. We don’t send technicians or quote; we’re a matchmaker that connects you with licensed Utah HVAC shops that serve your ZIP. Contractors who receive leads pay us.
