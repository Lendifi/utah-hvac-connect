Cold mornings along the Wasatch Front are hard on furnaces. Heating demand climbs from about 337 heating degree days in October to more than 1,000 in December and January at Salt Lake City International Airport (NOAA 1991–2020 normals), and about four in five homes in Salt Lake and Utah counties heat with utility gas (ACS 2024 1-year). When the furnace won’t start or the house won’t warm up, you usually need a licensed local shop.

Safety first

  • Gas smell: leave the home, then call 911 or Enbridge Gas’s emergency line, 1-800-767-1689, from outside. Don’t flip switches or use anything that could spark.
  • Carbon monoxide alarm: get everyone outside into fresh air and call 911. CO is colorless and odorless (EPA).
  • Smoke, burning smells, or scorching: turn the system off at the thermostat and call a licensed shop.

We don’t give safety or repair advice beyond pointing you to emergency services. Licensed shops do on-site assessment.

Basic checks furnace makers list before a service call

These are the simple, no-tools checks furnace manufacturer Carrier lists for a furnace that won’t turn on or won’t heat (Carrier: furnace not turning on, Carrier: troubleshooting a furnace):

  1. Thermostat: set to Heat, set a few degrees above the room temperature, and has power (replace batteries if the screen is blank).
  2. Furnace power switch: usually a light-style switch on or near the furnace. Make sure it’s on.
  3. Breaker: if the furnace breaker has tripped, reset it once. If it trips again, stop and call a shop.
  4. Air filter: a clogged filter can make a furnace overheat and shut itself off. Replace it if it’s dirty.

That’s where homeowner checks end. Don’t remove burner or control panels, bypass safety switches, or relight anything unless your furnace manual says to. If these checks don’t bring the heat back, it’s a job for a licensed technician.

What to tell the shop (it helps them come prepared)

  • What the furnace does when the thermostat calls for heat: nothing at all, clicks or hums, starts then shuts off, or the blower runs but the air is cold
  • Any blinking status light on the furnace and its pattern (count the flashes)
  • The furnace’s approximate age and the last time it was serviced
  • What you already checked from the list above
  • The indoor temperature now, and whether anyone at home is especially vulnerable to cold

Common “no heat” situations homeowners describe

  • Furnace won’t turn on after the first hard freeze
  • Blower runs but the air feels lukewarm or cold
  • Heat works in some rooms and not others
  • Unit short-cycles, shuts off, or needs frequent resets
  • Strange smells, unusual sounds, or ignition that won’t stay lit (describe these to a shop; don’t open panels)
  • You’re not sure whether it’s the furnace, the thermostat, or something else. That’s fine; pick “I’m not sure” on the form.

What should I do if I smell gas?

Leave the home first. Then, from outside, call 911 or Enbridge Gas’s emergency line at 1-800-767-1689. Don’t use light switches or anything that could spark. A furnace repair request is not the right channel for a gas leak.

Why is my furnace blowing cold air?

Carrier notes that if the fan runs but there’s no heat, the problem may be with the burner or ignition, and that a dirty filter can cause a safety shutoff from overheating. Checking the thermostat setting and the filter are homeowner steps; anything beyond that needs a licensed shop.

Why won’t my furnace turn on?

Manufacturers say the most common reasons are a thermostat setting, the furnace power switch being off, a tripped breaker, or a clogged filter. If those check out, ignition, flame-sensor, or control problems need a licensed technician.

Is no heat an emergency?

A gas smell or a carbon monoxide alarm is an emergency: get out and call for help. Otherwise, no heat in freezing weather is urgent for many households, so pick Today on the form. We can’t promise same-day service; shop schedules fill fast during cold snaps.
