No heat? Connect with licensed Utah furnace pros

Free match request for Salt Lake & Utah County homeowners — we introduce you to licensed shops; we don’t install or quote

Cold mornings along the Wasatch Front are hard on heaters. Heating demand in the Salt Lake valley is typically strongest from October through February, with the coldest stretch often in December and January (NOAA 1991–2020 normals for Salt Lake City International Airport: heating degree days jump from about 337 in October to over 1,000 in December and January). Most owner-occupied homes in Salt Lake and Utah counties heat with utility gas (about 79–80% per ACS 2024 1-year). When the furnace won’t start or the house won’t warm up, Utah homeowners usually need a licensed shop — not a national call center.

Utah HVAC Connect is a matchmaker. We connect you with licensed Utah HVAC contractors who serve your area. We are not a contractor, we don’t send technicians, and we don’t quote repairs or replacements.

Cold mornings, safety first

If you smell gas, hear a carbon monoxide alarm, or feel unsafe, treat that as an emergency and follow your local emergency guidance (leave the home if needed and contact emergency services or your utility). Working detectors and knowing your exits are homeowner responsibilities.

We are not a contractor and we don’t give safety, repair, or diagnostic advice. Licensed shops are the right place for on-site assessment. Our role is only to help you request a match with Utah-licensed pros who serve your ZIP.

Common “no heat” situations homeowners describe

These are situations people often mention on match requests — not a diagnostic guide and not DIY repair steps:

  • Furnace won’t turn on after the first hard freeze
  • Blower runs but air feels lukewarm or cold
  • Heat works in some rooms and not others
  • Unit short-cycles, shuts off, or needs frequent resets
  • Strange smells, unusual sounds, or a pilot/ignition that won’t stay lit (describe symptoms to a licensed shop — don’t open panels or bypass safety devices)
  • You’re not sure whether it’s the furnace, thermostat, or something else — that’s fine; pick “I’m not sure” on the form

A licensed Utah HVAC shop decides what to inspect and how to price the work. We don’t diagnose over the web.

Repair vs replace — how homeowners frame the conversation

Many winter calls start as “make it heat again.” Shops may discuss repair, temporary fixes, or replacement depending on age, parts, and your goals. That’s a conversation between you and the contractor — not advice from us.

On our form you can choose repair, replace, install, tune-up, or “I’m not sure.” If you know the system’s approximate age or whether you’re open to replacement quotes, sharing that helps shops respond usefully. We still don’t recommend repair vs replace ourselves.

How matching works when heat is urgent

  1. Tell us what you need on the free Get Help form — service type, urgency (for example Today or This week), ZIP, and how you prefer to be reached.
  2. We match licensed Utah shops that serve your area. We only work with Utah-licensed contractors; see contractor partners.
  3. Pros may reach out using the contact details you provide. You talk with them, compare what they offer, and hire who you want — or no one. No fee and no obligation for homeowners.

Winter urgency windows fill fast for shops. Clear ZIP, contact times, and urgency on the form help matching without implying we dispatch a tech or guarantee same-day service.
