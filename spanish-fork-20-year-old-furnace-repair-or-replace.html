A furnace that went in when a south Utah County home was built around 2001 is now about 25 years old. That’s a common situation here: Spanish Fork’s median home dates to 2001, and the 2000s were the biggest building decade in both Spanish Fork and Springville (see our Spanish Fork and Springville page). This guide is about what a furnace from that era probably is, which federal rules have changed since, and whether a dual-fuel heat pump belongs in the conversation given Spanish Fork’s cold nights and city power utilities. What a 2001-era furnace probably is

Federal rules set the floor. Non-weatherized gas furnaces made before November 19, 2015 had to meet an AFUE (annual fuel utilization efficiency) of at least 78%; from that date the minimum became 80% (10 CFR 430.32(e)). So a furnace installed around 2001 could be a non-condensing model near that floor, venting through a metal flue, or a 90%-plus condensing model with plastic intake and exhaust pipes. Non-condensing furnaces usually vent through a metal flue, while condensing models use plastic intake and exhaust pipes run to the outdoors (NREL ResStock documentation for DOE; Lennox EL195NE manual). The model number on the nameplate settles it; a technician can look it up.

  • Age guidance. ENERGY STAR suggests considering replacement when a furnace is more than 15 years old, and when a heat pump or AC is more than 10 (ENERGY STAR). Furnace makers commonly cite 15 to 20 years (American Standard). Age alone doesn’t decide it.
  • The AC is probably the same age, and may use R-22. Under EPA’s phaseout schedule, production and import of HCFC-22 (R-22) ended in 2020, after a decade in which new supply was limited to servicing equipment made before 2010 (EPA). The refrigerant is on the outdoor unit’s nameplate. Our early-2000s cost factors explain what that means for a repair.

The federal rule coming in 2028

In December 2023, DOE adopted a new standard for non-weatherized gas furnaces (Federal Register). As codified today, furnaces of that type manufactured on and after December 18, 2028 must reach at least 95% AFUE (10 CFR 430.32(e)(1)(iii); DOE). Two things to know:

  • It applies to new furnaces when they’re made. It doesn’t require anyone to replace a working furnace.
  • DOE notes that planned regulatory dates are preliminary and subject to change, so check the status if timing matters to your decision (DOE).

The practical effect for a 2001-era home with a metal flue: moving to a condensing furnace means new PVC venting, a condensate drain, and possibly a chimney liner for a water heater left on the old flue. Our venting checklist covers those questions.

How cold Spanish Fork nights get

NOAA’s normals station in Spanish Fork averages 118 nights a year with a low at or below freezing, 37 at or below 20°F, and about 8 at or below 10°F. January is the coldest month, with about 27.7 freezing nights and 12.6 nights at or below 20°F (NOAA monthly normals). That’s a little colder than the Provo BYU station:

Where dual fuel fits

A dual-fuel system pairs a heat pump with a gas furnace: the heat pump heats in milder weather and the furnace takes over when it gets cold. Enbridge Gas’s ThermWise rebate for dual fuel requires a changeover setpoint of 40°F or lower, an ENERGY STAR certified ducted heat pump, and an ENERGY STAR certified gas furnace of at least 95% AFUE (97.5% for Tier 2) (ThermWise). With 118 freezing nights a year in Spanish Fork, the furnace still carries the coldest hours under that kind of setup; the heat pump covers the milder ones. How the switch works is explained on our AC and heat pump page.

Why it comes up with a 25-year-old system: the furnace and AC are often due at the same time, so the choice isn’t only “which furnace.” It can be furnace-only, furnace plus a new AC, or furnace plus a heat pump. When we checked on November 9, 2026, ThermWise listed these dual-fuel rebates in Utah:

  • Tier 1: $1,000 with a newly installed gas furnace, or $700 where qualifying gas backup is already installed.
  • Tier 2 (higher heat pump and furnace ratings): $1,200 with a newly installed furnace, or $850 with existing gas backup (ThermWise).

One planning point from those rules: if you replace only the furnace now, a 95%-plus ENERGY STAR furnace keeps the “existing gas backup” tier open for a heat pump later. Ask your bidder whether the model they propose would qualify.

Utility check for south Utah County. Rocky Mountain Power’s Wattsmart dual-fuel rebate requires Rocky Mountain Power electricity (Wattsmart), and both Spanish Fork and Springville run their own power utilities, so it likely doesn’t apply. ThermWise applies only if Enbridge Gas is your gas utility. Springville’s power site points to UAMPS Smart Energy, where participation varies by city. Details and phone numbers are in the utilities section of our Spanish Fork and Springville page.

If you’re comparing cold-weather heat pump models, NEEP keeps a public list of cold-climate heat pump specifications (NEEP). Ask the bidder how much heat the proposed model delivers at low outdoor temperatures and what changeover temperature they recommend.

Repair or replace: questions for a 2001-era system

  • What failed, and is it a safety issue? A cracked heat exchanger is the big one. Ask the technician to show you.
  • Is it condensing or non-condensing, and what would a replacement do to the venting?
  • How old is the AC, and what refrigerant does it use? If it’s R-22 and also near the end of its life, ask for bids on the combined options.
  • Can I see three paths in writing: furnace-only, furnace plus AC, and dual fuel, each with model numbers?
  • Which rebates fit my utilities, and who files them?
  • Is the permit in the bid? Spanish Fork requires a permit for HVAC equipment, and Springville’s Building Division schedules inspections (see permits).

FAQ

Should I replace a 20-year-old furnace in Spanish Fork?

We can’t make that call; a licensed technician who sees it can. ENERGY STAR suggests considering replacement once a furnace is over 15 years old, and furnace makers commonly cite 15 to 20 years. Safety, repair history, and whether the AC is due too matter as much as age.

What efficiency is a furnace from around 2001?

Federal rules then required at least 78% AFUE for most gas furnaces, so it could be a non-condensing model near that level or a 90%-plus condensing model. Plastic intake and exhaust pipes usually mean condensing; a metal flue usually means non-condensing. The model number on the nameplate tells a technician which.

Will I have to replace my furnace because of the 2028 rule?

No. As codified today, the 95% AFUE minimum applies to non-weatherized gas furnaces manufactured on and after December 18, 2028. It doesn’t require replacing a working furnace. DOE says planned dates can change.

Does dual fuel make sense in Spanish Fork’s winters?

It’s one option to price. NOAA normals show about 118 nights a year at or below freezing in Spanish Fork, so the gas furnace still handles the coldest hours. ThermWise’s dual-fuel rebate requires the system to switch to the furnace at 40°F or lower.

Can I get the Wattsmart dual-fuel rebate in Spanish Fork or Springville?

Probably not. Wattsmart requires Rocky Mountain Power electricity, and both cities run their own power utilities. ThermWise rebates apply if Enbridge Gas is your gas utility. Check both bills.

Is my 2001 AC using R-22?

It may be. The refrigerant is listed on the outdoor unit’s nameplate. EPA ended production and import of R-22 in 2020, which matters if a repair needs refrigerant.
