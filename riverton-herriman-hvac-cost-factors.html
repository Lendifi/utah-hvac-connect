What drives HVAC costs in Riverton and Herriman homes

Why the same job means a different scope in a 2000s Riverton home and a 2010s Herriman home, with no prices. We match homeowners with licensed shops; we don’t quote.

By the Utah HVAC Connect editorial team · Published October 30, 2026 · Last updated October 30, 2026

TL;DR

  • No prices here, just scope. Riverton’s median home dates to 2001 and Herriman’s to 2013; 77% of Riverton’s homes predate 2010 versus 37% of Herriman’s (ACS 2020–2024).
  • Early-2000s homes: furnace and AC often age out together, the AC may use R-22 (EPA phaseout), and AHRI says a new system needs a matched indoor coil.
  • 2010s homes: check warranty first (parts, not labor), and expect the 2025 lower-GWP refrigerant change at the first AC replacement.
  • Herriman lists a $101 permit for a furnace and AC replaced together ($284 with a gas plan review); Riverton requires a permit but lists no flat HVAC fee.
  • ThermWise pays more when the furnace is new with the heat pump ($1,000–$1,200) than with an existing one ($700–$850).

About 77% of Riverton’s homes were built before 2010, compared with 37% of Herriman’s. Both cities are still adding homes: from April 2020 to July 2025, Herriman’s housing units grew from about 16,277 to 20,033 and Riverton’s from about 13,101 to 14,645 (Utah Population Committee 2025 estimates).

Your ZIP doesn’t tell you your era. By land area in the Census Bureau’s 2020 ZIP-to-place file, about 94% of Herriman is in 84096, but so is about 26% of Riverton; the rest of Riverton is in 84065 (Census relationship file). The year your home was built, and whether the furnace and AC are original, matter more than the ZIP.

Early-2000s homes (most of Riverton): what changes the scope

  • Furnace and AC often wear out together. In a home built in the late 1990s or 2000s with original equipment, both pieces are around the 15-to-20-year typical lifespan furnace makers cite (American Standard). Ask each bidder to price the furnace and AC separately and together, so you can see what doing both at once changes.
  • The AC may use R-22. Under EPA’s phaseout schedule, newly produced R-22 (HCFC-22) could be used from 2010 only to service equipment made before January 1, 2010, and production and import ended in 2020 (EPA HCFC phaseout). An original AC in a 2000s home may use it. The refrigerant is listed on the outdoor unit’s nameplate. A repair that needs refrigerant is a different conversation on an R-22 system.
  • A new AC usually means a new indoor coil. AHRI’s consumer guide says the indoor coil and outdoor unit are designed to work as a matched team. Written for the move away from R-22, it says a new system using the newer refrigerant needs both units replaced with a properly matched combination, and that replacing the indoor coil adds to installation cost (AHRI, A Perfect Match). Ask whether the coil is in the bid, and for the system’s AHRI reference number.
  • Venting and efficiency choices on the furnace. If your current furnace isn’t a condensing model, moving to 90%-plus efficiency changes the venting and drain work. Our furnace replacement guide covers that line item.
  • Rebates can favor doing both at once. ThermWise’s dual-fuel rebate is higher when the gas furnace is newly installed with the heat pump ($1,000 Tier 1, $1,200 Tier 2) than when an existing furnace stays as backup ($700 or $850), as listed on October 28, 2026 (ThermWise).

For the repair-or-replace decision itself, see our guide to original furnaces in 1990s–2001 homes.

2010s homes (most of Herriman): what changes the scope

  • Warranty status changes who pays for parts. In a newly built home, the furnace warranties we’ve read start at closing and offer 10 years of parts coverage if registered (5 if not), and they don’t cover labor. A furnace in a home that closed in 2015 reached year 10 in 2025. Find your closing date and registration first: how new-home furnace warranties work.
  • First AC replacements meet a refrigerant change. Under EPA’s Technology Transitions rules, new residential AC and heat pump systems must use refrigerants with a global warming potential below 700 starting January 1, 2025 (EPA). Because the indoor coil and outdoor unit are designed as a matched pair (AHRI), ask whether a bid replaces the coil, reuses it, and why.
  • Keeping a newer furnace and adding a heat pump. ThermWise’s “existing gas backup” dual-fuel tier requires an ENERGY STAR furnace of at least 95% AFUE (97.5% for Tier 2) and a changeover at 40°F or lower (ThermWise). Wattsmart lists $1,450 for an AHRI-rated dual-fuel system with a furnace of at least 80% or 90% AFUE depending on the heat pump’s rating, for existing homes on Rocky Mountain Power (Wattsmart). Whether your existing furnace qualifies is worth asking before you assume it can stay.
  • Two-story comfort can add scope. If upstairs and downstairs never match, dampers, return air, or zoning may come up in a bid; see upstairs hot, downstairs cold before you buy a bigger unit.
  • Townhomes: about 18% of Herriman’s housing units are single-family attached (ACS table B25024). If you’re in one, ask your HOA about rules for outdoor equipment before a contractor plans a new location.
  • Brand-new homes: Wattsmart’s dual-fuel rebate is for existing single-family homes, not new construction (Wattsmart).

Permit fees the cities publish

  • Herriman: repair and maintenance of existing heating and AC systems don’t need a permit, but replacing or adding a furnace or air conditioner does. A basic mechanical permit is $101, and a furnace and AC replaced at the same time can go on one permit for $101 total. Adding a gas appliance, such as an added gas furnace, needs a gas diagram and plan review, which adds a $183 plan-review fee ($284 total) and about 7–10 business days. Inspections are scheduled at 801-446-5327 (Herriman Electrical/Mechanical/Plumbing).
  • Riverton: the city requires a permit to install, alter, convert, or replace any mechanical or gas system regulated by code, and takes applications by email to building@rivertonutah.gov (Riverton Building Applications and Permits). Its building pages don’t list a flat HVAC permit fee, so ask the contractor what the permit line in the bid covers.
  • Bluffdale: addresses in 84065 that are in Bluffdale go through Bluffdale City.

These are city fees, not job prices. Most homeowners have the contractor pull the permit, so look for it as its own line on the bid.

Compare bids line by line

Get at least two written, itemized bids for the same scope, each with model numbers and the contractor’s license number. Then check that each one answers the questions for your home’s era:

  • Every home: how was it sized (a load calculation such as Manual J, or the old nameplate)? Is the permit included? Who files which rebate, and by when (ThermWise: six months; Wattsmart: 180 days)?
  • Early-2000s home: what refrigerant does the current AC use? Does the bid include the indoor coil? Furnace and AC separately and together? Venting changes?
  • 2010s home: is anything still under warranty? Is the coil replaced or reused, and why? Does the existing furnace meet the rebate’s AFUE rule if it stays?

Local housing data, utilities, and permit offices for both cities are on our Riverton, Bluffdale and Herriman page.

Check the license before work starts

Before anyone works on your system, look the company up on DOPL’s free Licensee Lookup & Verification. You want an Active status and an HVAC classification (H100, or the older S350), under the same business name that’s on your estimate. Step-by-step: how to check a Utah HVAC license.

FAQ

How much does it cost to replace a furnace or AC in Riverton or Herriman?

We don’t publish prices, because the real number depends on your home and the scope a licensed contractor sees. This guide covers what changes that scope, so you can compare itemized bids. The only figures we list are official rebates and city permit fees.

Why would a bid for a 2000s Riverton home include more work?

Original equipment in those homes is often near the end of its typical life at the same time, the AC may use R-22, and AHRI says a new system needs a matched indoor coil. Venting changes can also come up if the old furnace isn’t a condensing model.

What’s different in a 2010s Herriman home?

Warranty status comes first: furnace parts warranties in new homes start at closing and typically run 10 years if registered. First AC replacements now meet EPA’s 2025 lower-GWP refrigerant rules, so ask whether the coil is replaced.

How much is a Herriman HVAC permit?

Herriman lists $101 for a basic mechanical permit, and a furnace and AC replaced together can share one $101 permit. Adding a gas appliance adds a $183 plan-review fee, for $284 total. Repair and maintenance don’t need a permit.

Does a ZIP code tell me which era my home is from?

Not reliably. About a quarter of Riverton’s land is in 84096, the ZIP most of Herriman uses. Your home’s build year and whether the equipment is original matter more.
