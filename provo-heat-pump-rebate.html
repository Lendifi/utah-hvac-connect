Provo heat pump rebate: who qualifies and how to apply

Provo Power’s ducted and ductless rebates, the paperwork, and what to confirm before you sign. We match homeowners with licensed shops; we don’t quote or file rebates.

By the Utah HVAC Connect editorial team · Published October 30, 2026 · Last updated October 30, 2026

TL;DR

  • Provo Power customers: Provo City lists $1,000 for a ducted heat pump and $500 for ductless, applied for through RenewChoice’s Conserve program (as listed October 28, 2026).
  • Ducted: a conversion to a heat pump (not replacing one) that heats at least 80% of the home, 65,000 BTU/hr or less, AHRI-listed, HSPF2 7.5 or better.
  • Ductless: an AHRI-rated mini split of at least 20 SEER with an inverter; adding one to a gas-heated home can qualify.
  • Paperwork: itemized receipt, contractor invoice or permit final, and the AHRI certificate, filed within 180 days. About 4–6 weeks; the check goes to the account holder.
  • Confirm two things with the programs first: whether you can keep your gas furnace, and whether ThermWise’s dual-fuel rebate stacks.

If your electric bill comes from Provo Power, the city-owned utility, the heat pump rebate you’re looking for doesn’t come from Rocky Mountain Power. Provo City lists $1,000 for a ducted heat pump and $500 for a ductless heat pump, and it runs the applications through a program called Conserve on the RenewChoice website (Provo City; Provo Power programs). This guide goes through who qualifies, the paperwork, the one rule that’s worded differently in two places, and the questions to settle before you sign.

Utah HVAC Connect is a matchmaker. We connect homeowners with licensed Utah HVAC contractors; we’re not a contractor, and we don’t diagnose or quote. We aren’t affiliated with Provo City or RenewChoice and can’t approve a rebate. The program decides.

Who runs it, and who it’s for

  • Provo Power customers only. Provo’s “Programs and Rebates” page says the city’s power programs are consolidated on RenewChoice, and that the Conserve program covers heat pumps and water heaters (Provo Power programs). RenewChoice says that “currently, only Provo City is offering this rebate” (RenewChoice).
  • Existing homes. The ducted terms are for existing residential customers converting to a heat pump. Commercial buildings don’t qualify (Conserve terms).
  • One per electric account. A second rebate at the same address needs a separate account and meter, and the program’s FAQ says the same about apartments.
  • Not Wattsmart. Rocky Mountain Power’s Wattsmart heat pump rebates require that you buy your electricity from Rocky Mountain Power (Wattsmart), so they don’t fit Provo Power homes. If you’re in Orem or another Rocky Mountain Power city, see our heat pump rebate summary instead.

Not sure which utility you have? Look at the name on your electric bill. Our Provo and Orem page explains the split between the two cities.

Two details trip people up. The ducted rebate is for converting to a heat pump, so replacing an old heat pump with a new one doesn’t qualify. And the 80% rule means a small ducted heat pump serving one zone of a larger home may not count. The ductless rebate has no 80% rule, and the program’s FAQ says a home that keeps a gas furnace and adds a supplemental ductless unit can qualify for the $500 (Conserve terms and FAQ). For geothermal systems, the terms say to call first.

The gas-furnace question: confirm it before you sign

The two official pages describe the gas side differently, and we couldn’t settle it from the text alone:

  • Provo City’s rebate page says it offers rebates on electric appliances “when you dispose of your old gas-powered one” (Provo City).
  • RenewChoice describes the $1,000 as for a “dual fuel heat pump conversion,” and its terms are titled “High Efficiency & Dual Fuel Heat Pump.” In a dual-fuel system the gas furnace stays as backup. Its FAQ also says a gas furnace plus a supplemental ductless unit can qualify (RenewChoice; Conserve terms).

If you plan to keep your furnace as backup, ask Provo Power to confirm in writing that your project qualifies before you sign. Provo Power lists powerinfo@provo.gov and (801) 852-6852, and the city’s rebate page lists (801) 852-6802 for questions (Provo Power; Provo City).

How to apply, step by step

  1. Before you sign: confirm your utility is Provo Power, decide ducted or ductless, and settle the gas-furnace question above.
  2. Have a licensed contractor install it, and ask whether the city permit is in the bid. Provo’s Building page lists a building permit and an Equipment Installation Guide for furnace work, plus a site plan for an air conditioner (Provo Building). Ask whether your heat pump follows the AC steps.
  3. Collect the documents: an itemized receipt and the contractor invoice and/or proof of the city permit final inspection, plus the AHRI certificate for the installed system (Conserve terms).
  4. Apply online within 180 days of the conversion through RenewChoice.
  5. Wait about 4–6 weeks. The program says the check goes to the utility account holder.

Can you stack it with ThermWise?

Enbridge Gas’s ThermWise program, which serves Provo’s gas customers, lists separate dual-fuel heat pump rebates, as listed on October 28, 2026 (ThermWise):

  • Tier 1: $700 with an existing gas backup furnace, or $1,000 with a newly installed one. It requires an ENERGY STAR ducted heat pump (HSPF2 7.5 or better, SEER2 13.3 or better), an ENERGY STAR furnace of 95% AFUE or higher, and a changeover to the furnace at 40°F or lower.
  • Tier 2: $850 with an existing furnace, or $1,200 with a new one, for higher-efficiency equipment (HSPF2 8.5, SEER2 17.1, EER2 10.9, and a 97.5% AFUE furnace).
  • ThermWise wants the request postmarked within six months of installation, which is a different clock from Provo’s 180 days.

Neither program page we read says whether its rebate can be combined with the other. Ask both before you count on both. ThermWise’s hotline is 1-877-932-0610. The federal 25C tax credit isn’t allowed for equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025 (IRS).

Which Provo homes this fits

About 74% of Provo’s 34,022 occupied homes heat with utility gas, 23% with electricity, and about 3% with bottled or tank gas such as propane (ACS 2020–2024, table B25040). The Conserve FAQ says homes heated with a wood stove or propane can qualify. It doesn’t address homes that already heat with electric resistance (baseboards or an electric furnace), so ask the program if that’s your house.

About 61% of Provo’s occupied homes are rented (ACS table B25003). Because the rebate is one per electric account and the check goes to the account holder, renters and landlords should agree on who applies before the work starts.

Questions for every bid

  • Is this a conversion to a heat pump under Conserve’s terms, and will it heat at least 80% of the home (ducted)?
  • What’s the AHRI reference number, and does the system meet HSPF2 7.5 (ducted), or 20 SEER with an inverter compressor and variable-speed indoor fan (ductless)?
  • Is the outdoor unit 65,000 BTU/hr or smaller, and how did you size it? Ask whether the shop ran a load calculation.
  • Is the permit and final inspection included, and will you give me the paperwork the rebate needs?
  • If I keep my furnace, how is the changeover set, and does it meet ThermWise’s 40°F rule?

For the bigger choice between AC-only, dual fuel, and all-electric, see AC and heat pump replacement in Utah. Lehi has its own city power utility too; our Lehi City Power vs Rocky Mountain Power guide compares its rebates.

Check the license before work starts

The ducted rebate requires a licensed HVAC contractor. Before anyone works on your system, look the company up on DOPL’s free Licensee Lookup & Verification. You want an Active status and an HVAC classification (H100, or the older S350), under the same business name that’s on your estimate. Step-by-step: how to check a Utah HVAC license.

FAQ

How much is the Provo heat pump rebate?

As listed on October 28, 2026: $1,000 for a ducted heat pump conversion and $500 for a qualifying ductless heat pump, one rebate per residential electric account. The program says terms are subject to change.

Who can get it?

Provo Power residential customers with an account that’s current (not more than 30 days past due). RenewChoice says only Provo City offers this rebate. Rocky Mountain Power customers use Wattsmart instead.

Do I have to remove my gas furnace?

The two official pages are worded differently. Provo City’s page mentions disposing of the old gas-powered appliance, while RenewChoice calls the $1,000 a dual-fuel conversion and says a gas furnace plus a supplemental ductless unit can qualify. Confirm with Provo Power before you sign.

What documents do I need?

An itemized receipt and contractor invoice and/or proof of the city permit final inspection, plus the AHRI certificate. Apply online through RenewChoice within 180 days of the conversion.

How long does the rebate take?

The program lists about 4–6 weeks for processing, and the check goes to the utility account holder.

Can I replace my old heat pump and get the rebate?

Not the ducted one: the terms say upgrades to an existing heat pump don’t qualify. The ductless rebate also isn’t offered when replacing or upgrading a ducted heat pump.

Do you apply for the rebate or install the heat pump?

No. We’re a matchmaker: we connect homeowners with licensed Utah HVAC shops that serve their ZIP, and we don’t install, quote, or file rebates. The request is free, with no obligation to hire.
