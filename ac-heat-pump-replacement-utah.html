Why look at AC in October?

Salt Lake City averages about 1,420 cooling degree days a year, with July (500) and August (441) doing most of the work, compared with about 5,154 heating degree days (NOAA annual normals; monthly). Utah County is similar: Provo’s normals show about 1,205 cooling and 4,968 heating degree days (NOAA, Provo BYU station). Two things follow:

  • Shops are busiest in July and August. If your AC limped through this summer, fall and spring are calmer times to get bids.
  • A heat pump is also a heater. In a heating-heavy climate, the AC decision and the furnace decision are often the same decision. See our furnace replacement guide.

Dual fuel: why it shows up in so many Utah bids

Rocky Mountain Power describes dual fuel this way: in summer the heat pump works like a high-efficiency central AC, in spring and fall it provides efficient heating, and in the coldest months the gas furnace takes over only when needed (Wattsmart). Both Utah utilities list rebates for it, and Enbridge Gas’s ThermWise rebate requires the system to switch to the furnace at 40°F or lower (ThermWise). The two utilities have promoted dual fuel together before (SWEEP case study).

Questions to ask every bidder

  • How did you size it? Ask whether the shop ran a load calculation (often called Manual J) or matched the old unit’s size.
  • Is the outdoor and indoor equipment a matched, AHRI-certified system? Wattsmart’s higher dual-fuel rebate requires an AHRI-rated system.
  • How does it perform in cold weather? For cold-climate models, ask whether the unit is on the NEEP cold-climate heat pump list.
  • Are you a Wattsmart participating contractor, and who files the rebates? Wattsmart requires a program-participating contractor and an application within 180 days.
  • Does my electrical panel need work? Is the permit included?
  • Which refrigerant does the quoted system use? (See below.)

Refrigerant changes: what to ask

Under EPA’s Technology Transitions rules, new residential and light-commercial AC and heat pump systems must use refrigerants with a global warming potential below 700 starting January 1, 2025. Systems whose components were all made or imported before that date have no installation deadline (EPA). In practice, bids may include either kind. Ask which refrigerant each quoted system uses, and compare like with like.

FAQ

Do you install or quote AC and heat pumps?

No. Utah HVAC Connect is a matchmaker. We connect homeowners with licensed Utah HVAC contractors. We don’t sell equipment, install, diagnose, or quote.

Can I replace just my AC and keep my furnace?

Often, yes. Replacing only the outdoor AC (and usually the matching indoor coil) is a common path when the furnace is newer or in good shape. A licensed shop confirms the new AC is matched to your furnace and ductwork.

Does a heat pump work in Utah winters?

Heat pumps are designed to heat and cool, and cold-climate models are built for lower outdoor temperatures. In Utah, many bids pair a heat pump with a gas furnace (dual fuel), so the furnace takes over in the coldest weather. Rocky Mountain Power and Enbridge Gas both list dual-fuel rebates.

What rebates are there for heat pumps in Utah in 2026?

As listed on October 7, 2026: Rocky Mountain Power’s Wattsmart program lists up to $1,450 for an AHRI-rated dual-fuel system ($700 if not AHRI-rated), and Enbridge Gas ThermWise lists $700–$1,200 for qualifying dual-fuel systems. Wattsmart’s all-electric heat pump rebates mainly cover homes converting from electric heat. Conditions apply, so confirm with each program.

Is there still a federal tax credit for heat pumps?

Not for 2026 installs. The IRS says the section 25C credit isn’t allowed for property placed in service after December 31, 2025.

When is the best time to replace an AC in Utah?

Cooling demand peaks in July and August, so shops are busiest then. If your system still runs, getting bids in spring or fall gives you more time to compare. A heat pump also heats, so fall is a reasonable time to look at one.

What refrigerant will a new system use?

Under EPA rules, new residential AC and heat pump systems built or imported from January 1, 2025 must use refrigerants with a global warming potential below 700. Older-refrigerant equipment made before then can still be installed. Ask each shop which refrigerant the quoted system uses.
